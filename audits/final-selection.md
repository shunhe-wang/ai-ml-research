# First-project selection decision

**Decision date:** 2026-07-17
**Selected project:** F1, in its narrowed form
**Working title:** *Do Prior Decisions Carry Over? Causal Decomposition of Sequential History Effects in Rule-Bound Language Model Decisions*
**Status:** Selected for protocol and no-cost smoke implementation; confirmatory execution remains gated.

## Decision

Select the narrowed F1 project:

> **When the governing rules and target case are held byte-identical and fully intact, does the same exact decision shift after positive versus negative histories of independently complete same-rule cases, beyond token-, position-, role-, and format-matched neutral history? Which observable history channel and boundary operation explains any excess?**

The primary contribution is a paired causal estimate of **directional cross-case carryover**, not discovery of sequential interference, context pollution, legal anchoring, cognitive overload, or reset mitigation. Those broad claims are already occupied.

F1 earns selection because it is the only audited candidate that retains a testable gap, an identifiable primary estimand, a fully synthetic and independently checkable data path, a meaningful null, and a realistic solo implementation path. It does not depend on private data, human-subject recruitment, external expert annotation, proprietary generator replay, or historical-law interpretation.

## Why F2 is not selected

The hostile venue reviewer ranked F2 first because a successful bias-and-coverage audit fits NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets or TMLR cleanly. The implementation and statistical audits exposed two load-bearing failures:

1. empirical coverage is not identified from repeated generator draws on fixed seeds without an outer known-truth reference population or simulation; and
2. the novel validity-bias component requires independent semantic adjudication at a scale that violates the solo-project and no-unapproved-external-labor constraints.

Using one generator, pre-generated variants, deterministic-only tasks, or a cluster bootstrap on one dataset would remove the very component that survives the literature audit. F2 is therefore rejected as the first project, not shrunk into an incremental note. It remains a plausible later collaboration.

## Why F3 is not selected

F3's original question is directly occupied by *Asking For An Old Friend* (ICAIL 2026). Its causal temporal-applicability pivot is cleaner but is a new project assembled from close components. The effective sample size is the number of independently substantive rule migrations, not the number of generated cases. A credible real-rule tier also needs independent verification of historical text, effective dates, transition rules, and two executable implementations. The pivot fails the first-project scope and literature-gap gates.

## Responses to the strongest F1 objections

### “It is just all the controls prior work omitted.”

The paper will rise or fall on one directional estimand: whether an identical target shifts between positive and negative prior-outcome histories under matched load. Channel and boundary modules are tests of predeclared discriminating predictions, not a claim that control completeness itself is novel. A generic degradation curve is a rejection outcome.

### “The factorial will explode.”

The protocol will use two staged modules rather than a Cartesian product: source/channel attribution, then boundary operations applied to one predeclared history type and neutral control. One primary contrast is frozen. A design simulation, not repeated model calls, determines the number of independent schemas and targets.

### “Self-rationale effects are confounded.”

The primary claim does not identify private self-recognition or commitment. Model-generated history is an operational secondary treatment over a frozen transcript distribution. Any source/role claim requires cross-feeding byte-identical records under controlled assistant-versus-quoted-user placement. Otherwise the paper reports exposure to model-generated records and no more.

### “Synthetic rules have no ecological validity.”

The claim concerns causal model behavior under independently complete rule decisions, for which executable synthetic worlds provide necessary internal validity. The design uses multiple independently specified schemas in at least two non-mathematical worlds and does not generalize prevalence to law, medicine, or real institutions. A later external validation is follow-up work, not hidden scope.

### “A null would be uninteresting.”

A powered paired null with predeclared equivalence bounds would show that prior findings from sharded tasks, subjective admissions, math sequences, and natural chats do not transfer at a practically meaningful magnitude to independently complete exact-rule cases under the tested horizons and model panel. A ceiling/floor null or an imprecise failure to reject is not publishable and triggers rejection.

## Kill gates before confirmatory execution

Selection authorizes protocol and free local smoke work only. Stop or redesign before confirmatory data if any gate fails:

1. a final forward/backward citation search finds the same joint paired design;
2. two independently structured solvers disagree on any frozen primary case or rule trace;
3. the renderer cannot keep target/rule bytes intact and history token/role/position structure within the predeclared model-specific tolerance;
4. disjoint calibration cases place most required model families above 95% or below 50%, with no predeclared recalibration path;
5. simulation shows that the staged design cannot estimate the smallest effect of scientific interest with acceptable type-I error, coverage, and power at an affordable number of independent schemas/targets;
6. the design requires one rule world, fewer than three model families, repeated calls in place of independent targets, or post-outcome selection of the “worst” history;
7. the no-cost smoke run cannot reproduce injected null and non-null effects through the full generate-render-run-score-analyze path; or
8. the minimum credible confirmatory tier requires spending that has not received explicit approval.

## Runner-up and contingency

F2 remains the substantive runner-up but is not an operational fallback because its hard-gate failures are structural. The nearest lower-ranked contingency is C06, audit-complete reproduction of a multi-agent judging pipeline; it receives only a bounded direct-artifact screen and will not displace F1 unless that screen reveals a cleaner gap and F1 fails a kill gate.

## What selection does not authorize

It does not authorize paid model calls, cloud compute, external annotation, outreach, submission, publication, or public release. It does authorize building the synthetic generator, dual solvers, renderers, schemas, tests, simulations, local open-model smoke path, and frozen protocol.
